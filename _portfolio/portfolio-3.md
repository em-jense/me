*eternity* is a interactive essay/story I made with Twine, inspired by works like [*Your Lover Has Turned Into a Flock of Birds*](https://ifdb.org/viewgame?id=icbrwvr7mehr4hl8) by Miranda Simon and [*Something Blue*](https://ifdb.org/viewgame?id=vonih848rprc4q6v) by Emery Joyce, focusing on a through narrative of a fictionalized lesbian woman, Bette, and her life, career, and relationship with a woman named Lynne. The gameplay of the story follows the player being given the task of sorting through a few records of a collection, with each choice taking them down a different path towards how this record will ultimately be catalogued and sorted in the archive. Some choices will lead to a more positive outcome, while some may result in the player (even in unintentionally or unknowingly) contributing to the erasure of queer voices in the archive. This aspect of the game is in part inspired by artists like Danielle Brathwaite-Shirley where failing is part of the point.

This story is in part inspired from a mini collection of little love notes that were found in a book that was for a booksale I helped out with, which documented part of an exchange between a woman named Lynne and a woman named Linda in 1994. My friends and I had taken a look at the letters, and we couldn't help but think about the fact that this love persisted even in a time where they had to pass secret notes tucked into books, with words of love written in the margins of a library bookmark. And I couldn't help but think about where they are now, together or apart. 

This essay/story is thus also a product of my own personal academic interests in critical archival studies and queer theory, particularly around how queer people have historically been excluded from archival records or have had their records pathologized or quietly tucked away from view, either by physical organization or intellectual organization. The original painting (styles to look like a photo that might be in this fictional collection) I included is based on a photo I found in the [ArQuives's](https://digitalexhibitions.arquives.ca/exhibits/show/queeringfamilyphotography/item/975) database and the oral history transcript is similarly inspired by oral history projects done by the ArQuives and other community archive projects. The work also engages with a critique of institutional archives more directly, like the City of Vancouver Archives, whose real subject terms I used alongside terms that do not exist in their database as a means to show what is and is not often included even now. Within the story there are also hints towards social views of queerness overtime, compulsory heterosexuality, and the social constraints of queerness both then and now.
